How to use this checker

Follow these five steps in order. Each step turns green when it's done.

  1. 1
    Choose your current year

    This unlocks the checker and applies year rules, like RESMETO being open only from your 3rd year.

  2. 2
    Tick the courses you've passed

    On the flowchart, tap the small circle on each course you have passed. Leave everything else unticked; courses you haven't passed are on record with the Registrar's Office. If a course needs a prerequisite you haven't ticked, the checker will ask you first. If you're enrolled this term, open each of those courses and tap Enrolled now, then set the term under "Currently enrolled."

  3. 3
    Record your electives

    Under "Choices for electives," tap I've taken it for electives you passed, or Add to plan for ones you want to take. Do this for your AMELECs and your General Education electives (GELECT2 and GELECT3). Then fill in My elective wish list with when you plan to take each remaining elective and your 1st, 2nd, and 3rd choices. Music Production electives are 2 units each, so you need two of them for one AMELEC.

    Not sure an elective will count? Check it first with the CIM Electives Self-Assessment Tool ↗ (Google Form by the CIM Program Chairperson).

  4. 4
    Fix anything flagged

    A red ! means something needs attention, like an elective that won't count. Open "Check these" and correct each item.

  5. 5
    Download and send your plan

    Save your results as a PDF for advising, then send your elective wish list to the CIM program so it can plan which electives to offer.

      Rules to know before you enroll

      From the Benilde Student Handbook (A.Y. 2025–2030) and the ABCIM Program Specification. Section numbers point to the handbook.

      Open the Benilde Student Handbook (A.Y. 2025–2030)
      Follow your flowchart and get your adviser's approval

      Take courses in the order shown on your flowchart. Underloading, dropping, withdrawing, or taking a leave of absence can delay your graduation past your Maximum Residency Period (3.6).

      Your Academic Adviser must approve your courses before you enroll or adjust in any term, and any change to your enrollment also needs your adviser's approval (5.2).

      General Education electives

      You take three General Education electives, and only the listed courses count:

      GELECT1 (Social Sciences and Philosophy): REEXECO, Religious Experience and Spirituality, Term 3. This one is fixed.

      GELECT2 (Math, Science, and Technology): choose one, ECOLSUS (Ecology and Sustainability) or DSRMNGT (Disaster Management, also written DISMNGT), Term 5.

      GELECT3 (Arts and Humanities): choose one, LITSTOR (The Art of Storytelling) or COMNMED (Communication in the New Media), Term 8.

      Electives: what counts

      AMELEC 1–3 must be about the theory or history of a selected art form or related industries. AMELEC 4–6 must be about the production of a selected art form. Both are free choice with restrictions: only courses on the approved list are credited. They can be taken from SACP, SED, and SNMA.

      Music Production electives are 2 units each, so two are needed for one AMELEC. AMCURTP, AMINFOA, AMTALEN, AMDIGIT, and AMGASTR are for students enrolled from AY 2023–2024 onwards.

      Not sure an elective will count? Check it with the CIM Electives Self-Assessment Tool ↗.

      Course load, overload, and underload

      Your regular load is the one on your flowchart (3.6.1). An overload may be allowed for students with excellent academic performance or those graduating at the end of the term, enrolled during the adjustment period only. Graduating students may take up to 24 units (3.6.2).

      If you end up underloaded because a course isn't offered, consult your Academic Adviser and, if possible, add units during the adjustment period (3.6.3).

      Special classes and cross-enrollment

      Only students graduating at the end of the term may request a special class, and only when a required course isn't offered, an old-curriculum course has no substitute, the course is a prerequisite that didn't reach the minimum enrollees, or for other meritorious cases decided by the Registrar. File the request with your program before the term begins (3.8).

      Cross-enrollment to another school is allowed only if the course isn't regularly offered at Benilde and no special class is available. Get a Cross-Enrollment Permit from the Registrar at least one week before pre-enrollment (3.15).

      Dropping and withdrawing

      Dropping is discouraged. Courses dropped in the first two weeks won't appear on your Transcript of Records, but retention fees apply: 25% in week 1, 50% in week 2, and no refund after that (3.9).

      Withdrawal from week 3 to week 5 shows as "W" on your transcript, with 100% tuition retention. Excessive absences are not a valid reason to withdraw; you'll get an R instead (3.10).

      Attendance

      Missing more than 20% of total course hours means a failing grade (R or F) and no credit (5.6.1). That is 6 or more absences in a class that meets twice a week, or 3 or more in a class that meets once a week (5.6.7).

      Arriving within the first 20 minutes of a 1-hour class, or 30 minutes of a 1.5-hour or longer class, counts as late, which is half an absence. Arriving after that counts as absent (5.6.3–5.6.4).

      Maximum Residency Period (MRP), including shiftees and transferees

      What the MRP is. The MRP is the longest time you're allowed to finish your degree: 1.5 times the number of terms on your flowchart (5.4.16.1.1). The CIM flowchart has 9 terms, so 1.5 × 9 = 13.5 terms. Ask the Registrar's Office to confirm your exact MRP end term.

      What counts toward it. Every term from when you started, including terms on Leave of Absence (LOA) or Absence Without Leave (AWOL) (5.4.16.1.3, 3.11). Underloading, dropping, withdrawing, and failing courses can all push your graduation later (3.6).

      What happens when it runs out. You can't be readmitted once your MRP expires, and reaching the end of it is a ground for separation from the College (5.4.16.1.2, 3.20.2.1).

      Extensions. An MRP extension may be granted, but you must keep good disciplinary standing; a major offense during the extension can revoke it (5.4.16.1.5). If you're returning from LOA or AWOL and may go past your MRP, get the extension approved before you re-enroll (3.12).

      If you are a shiftee. You can only shift if you have enough terms left in your MRP to finish CIM without needing an extension (3.13.1.3). You need at least 2 terms in your previous program before shifting, and you can shift only once (3.13). Grades from your previous Benilde program are included in your CGPA for honors (6.3.5). Your MRP is generally counted from when you first entered Benilde, so confirm your remaining terms with the Registrar's Office.

      If you are a transferee. Apply for credit transfer by the end of your 2nd term; no credit transfer is granted after that (3.17). At most 20% of the degree's units can be credited, and all major and institutional courses must be taken at Benilde (3.17.1.6–3.17.1.7). For CIM, transferred courses need a grade of at least 2.0 (80%). Credited units don't carry grades into your CGPA, but failing marks or withdrawals from your previous school disqualify you from honors (3.17.1.9). Ask the Registrar's Office how your credited units affect your MRP.

      What to do now.

      1. Use this checker to mark everything you've passed, including credited courses.
      2. Count the terms you've already used, including any LOA or AWOL terms.
      3. Compare your remaining courses with your remaining terms. Watch the prerequisite chains (AMINTRO, RESMETO → PRACRE1 → PRACRE2, AMLANG1 → AMLANG2) because these can't be taken in the same term.
      4. If it looks tight, book a consultation with your Academic Adviser early, before you need an overload, special class, or extension.
      Graduation requirements

      Besides all academic courses, you must complete non-academic and institutional requirements: retreats and recollections, the Initial Interview, CSBWELL (ID 125 onwards), the Exit Survey Form, an online resume on the DLS-CSB Career Portal, and any restorative discipline or community service obligations. Clearances are due the week before final exams of your last term (6.1).
